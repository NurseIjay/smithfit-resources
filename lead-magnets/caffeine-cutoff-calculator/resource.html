Part 3

The rotating-week reset

For the flip from nights back to days (or any switch that scrambles you). Five moves, in order, with your clock times.

1
Anchor with a short sleep, not a full day.
A full day of sleep keeps you on night time for days. A 3 to 4 hour anchor pays the worst of the debt and still lets tonight happen.
2
3
Daylight and a 10-minute walk instead of the afternoon refill.
Light and movement are the two signals that actually move your body clock. Coffee is not one of them.
4
One real meal with 25 to 40 g of protein, and a big bottle of water. A cup of shredded rotisserie chicken is 35 g on its own.
A tired body reads as a hungry body. Protein and water quiet most of the fake hunger and the fake fatigue.
5
In bed near your normal time tonight, and expect one rough evening.
That rough evening is the flip working, not you failing. You buy back your whole week with one honest bedtime.
Part 4

When the plan breaks

Coaching is what happens when the plan breaks. Two cards: the wall you used to answer with coffee, and the short-sleep emergency.

After last call: the 4am-instead protocol
Run it in this order. Works the same on a 2:00 pm day-shift slump.
1
Bright light, wherever you can get it. Med room, supply room, 5 minutes.
Light is the strongest wake signal your body has, and it has zero half-life. Nothing to clear at bedtime.
2
10 minutes of movement. A stair lap or a brisk loop of the unit.
Movement buys you about an hour of alertness and wears off on purpose, which is exactly what you want at 4:00 am.
3
16 oz of cold water. Before you judge how tired you are.
Mild dehydration reads as exhaustion. A lot of 4:00 am fog is thirst wearing a fatigue costume.
4
A 15 to 20 g protein snack. One Greek yogurt cup (5.3 oz, 15 g) or 2 hard-boiled eggs plus a string cheese (about 19 g).
Steady fuel, no spike and crash, and it holds you to the drive home.
