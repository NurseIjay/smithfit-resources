Part 2

The 3-nights-in-a-row plan

The stretch that decides your week. Same math, held for three nights, with the one adjustment most nurses miss on the last one.

Night 1

Front-load it

Your biggest cups live in the first half of the shift, when they help the most and cost the least. Last call stays at --. After that, switch to the swaps: water and electrolytes, a 10-minute walk, a protein snack.

Night 2

Hold the line

Night 2 is where the drive-home cup sneaks in, because you are running on yesterday's short sleep. Same last call, --, no negotiating. One more cup tonight is one less hour of the sleep that gets you through night 3.

Night 3

Pull it one hour earlier

The sleep after your last night is the sleep that resets your whole week, so protect it hardest. Last call moves to --. The back half of night 3 runs on the swaps and the finish line.

The morning after night 3: sleep a short recovery block of 3 to 4 hours instead of a full day, get up, get daylight and a short walk, eat a real meal, and go to bed close to your normal time. You flip back in one day instead of three.
Part 3

The rotating-shift reset card

For the flip from nights back to days (or any switch that scrambles you). Five moves, in order.

1
Anchor with a short sleep, not a full day. After your last night shift, sleep 3 to 4 hours and set an alarm. A full day of sleep keeps you on night time for days.
2
One real cup when you wake up. Then treat the flip day like a day shift: last call around --, even though you will want a rescue cup at 5:00 pm. Especially then.
3
Daylight and a 10-minute walk instead of the afternoon refill. Light and movement are the two signals that actually move your body clock. Coffee is not one of them.
4
Protein and water on the flip day. A tired body reads as a hungry body. A protein snack and a big bottle of water quiet most of the fake hunger and the fake fatigue.
5
Expect one rough evening. That is the flip working, not you failing. Go to bed near your normal time, and tomorrow feels human again.
