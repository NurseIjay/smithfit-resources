Set it for tonight

Your first night, your middle nights and your last night are three different bodies. Treating them the same is why the week feels random.

    The 3am rule

    3am is not a willpower problem. It is the hour your blood sugar bottoms out, your cortisol is at its lowest, and the only thing open is a machine. Nothing you do at 3am fixes 3am. The meal before your shift is what fixes 3am.

    The night bag

    Pack it once on your first night and it carries the whole stretch. It remembers what you have ticked.

    Why the sunglasses are on the list

    They are not a nice extra. Morning light on the drive home tells your brain the day has started, and it will fight your sleep for hours afterwards. Sunglasses on before you leave the building, not once you are squinting in the car park.

    The nights that do not go to plan

    Find yours. Do the one thing.

    Eat standing up at the first gap, even if it is ninety seconds and even if it is hour ten.

    The gap is what wrecks you, not the setting. A rushed meal at hour ten beats no meal, and it stops the drive home from turning into a kitchen raid.

    Nuts or a bar with 15g or more of protein, and a full bottle of water first.

    Drink first. Dehydration at 3am feels exactly like hunger, and half the time the water settles it before the food even matters.

    Add one protein feed around hour thirteen and drop the caffeine idea entirely.

    Caffeine at hour thirteen buys you nothing and costs you the sleep you desperately need. Food buys you the rest of the shift.

    Blackout, cold room, and do not lie there past twenty minutes. Get up, dim light, and try again in thirty.

    Lying awake trains your body that bed is where you are anxious. Breaking the loop matters more than the hour you eventually get.

    Eat on the clock tonight, not on appetite, and skip the workout without a second thought.

    On two hours of sleep your hunger cues are lying to you in both directions. The plan is the plan, and training tonight takes more than it gives.

    Count from your last full day off. First night back is a first night, whatever the calendar says.

    Your body counts from the last time it slept normally, not from the schedule. That is the whole logic of pattern clocks.
