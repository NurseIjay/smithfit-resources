Set your sleep time

Same order every time. The order is what does the work, not any single item on the list.

    Start with two, not eight

    Nobody adopts this whole list in one night. Pick the shower and the box breathing. Those two carry most of the effect, they are hard to forget, and once they are automatic the rest attaches to them by itself.

    Before you judge yourself on this

    Sleep is the habit most likely to make you feel like you are failing, and it should not be. I do not expect you to be checking that box in your first month.

    If you are dealing with a thyroid issue, PCOS, perimenopause, an autoimmune thing, or just years of nights, your sleep system is one of the broken pieces we are here to repair. It is not a test you are supposed to pass in week two. We work from the inside out, and sleep is downstream of almost everything else we are fixing.

    On the weeks it does not happen, your check-in still tells me. Water retention, cravings, mood, a flat scale with a rough week behind it. That is your body talking, and I can coach from it whether or not the box got ticked.

    What actually improves first

    Not the hours. The falling asleep gets faster, and the 3am wake-ups get shorter. Most people notice those two weeks before the total ever moves, and both of them are worth more than an hour of extra broken sleep.

    The nights it does not work

    Find yours. Do the one thing.

    Get up. Dim light, no screen, eight cycles of box breathing, and back to bed in twenty minutes.

    Wired and tired is cortisol still running the show. You cannot force your way past it, but you can downshift out of it, and lying there just deepens the association between bed and frustration.

    Look at your last meal and your alcohol, not your bedtime.

    A 3am wake is usually blood sugar or alcohol clearing, and both are fixable in the evening rather than at 3am. Eating a bit more at dinner, especially protein, is the first thing to try.

    Sunglasses tomorrow, from the moment you leave the building.

    Nine times out of ten it is the drive home. Light on the drive tells your brain the day started, and no amount of blackout curtain undoes a signal it already received.

    Keep two items and drop the rest. The shower and the breathing, wherever they fit.

    A short routine you do every night beats a perfect one you do twice. Consistency is the mechanism, the length never was.

    Keep it, but put it across the room and turn the brightness to the lowest setting.

    I would rather you keep something that works for you than fail a rule. The distance matters more than the screen, because it is the 3am reach that costs you the second half of the night.

    Tell me, and bring your last two check-ins.

    Sometimes sleep is not a routine problem. Thyroid, iron, perimenopause and sleep apnoea all live here, and that is a conversation, not a page.
