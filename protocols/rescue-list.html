The shelf rule

If it lives at home, it is not a rescue, it is a nice idea. Rescue food lives where the emergency happens: your bag, your locker, your glovebox. Restock the same day each week so you never find out you are empty at the moment you need it.

When the day goes sideways

Do the one thing. Do not read the rest.

Roll-ups, standing up, ninety seconds, right now. Do not wait for a real break, it is not coming today.

Eating standing up at hour five beats eating perfectly at hour twelve, every time. The gap is what wrecks the next four hours, not the setting.

Nuts, or a bar with 15g or more of protein, and a bottle of water. Skip the candy and the energy drink even though both are calling you.

Candy spikes you fast and drops you harder. An energy drink after 2pm takes your sleep with it, which takes tomorrow with it.

Grilled protein, whatever green thing they have, sauce on the side. Any restaurant, any drive-through, that order exists.

You do not need the perfect menu. You need protein and something with fibre. That is the whole decision.

Eat your next meal on time and normally. That is the entire correction.

The pizza was never the problem. Skipping the next two meals to make up for it is the problem, because that is what turns one thing into a three-day spiral.

The shake. Ninety seconds, and liquid goes down when food will not.

No hunger cue on shift is not a sign you do not need food. It is your stress response turning the signal off. Eat to the clock on those days, not to appetite.

Protein first, ten minutes, before anything else. Then decide what you actually want for dinner.

Starving decisions are not decisions. Ten minutes of protein puts your brain back in the room and the evening stops being a fight.

The shake, then sleep. That is the whole plan and it counts.

A day where you got protein in and slept is a win, not a failure with a nice story attached. This was never built for the perfect weeks.

Know your danger window

Same protocol, different hours. One planned bite before the window opens is worth more than any decision made inside it.

Nights and rotating

Roughly 2am to 5am. Your blood sugar bottoms out and the only thing open is a machine. Eat something with protein before midnight even if you are not hungry, because that one decision is what stops the 3am raid. Your last real intake lands a couple of hours before you sleep, not on the drive home.

Days and everything else

Roughly 3pm to 6pm. The day has drained you and dinner is still hours out. A rescue at 3pm is what keeps 7pm from becoming a standing-in-the-kitchen situation. Eat before the drive home, not after you walk in the door.

When food is not the problem

Sometimes the 3am raid is not hunger, it is a nervous system that has been on since 7pm. If you can name the patient or the moment that set you off, do a Chaos Dose first: box breathing, four cycles, about a minute. Then decide if you are hungry. Half the time the answer changes.
